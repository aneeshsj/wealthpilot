# Sector Underwriting Note: Textiles

**ASHVA CAPITAL INTERNAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL**
**SECTION 14: SECTOR-SPECIFIC UNDERWRITING PROTOCOLS**
**SUB-SECTION 14.4: TEXTILE MANUFACTURING AND WHOLESALE (SME)**

---

### 14.4.1 Applicability and Scope
This sub-section governs all credit facilities extended to Small and Medium Enterprise (SME) borrowers operating within the textile sector, including spinning mills, weaving units, garment manufacturers, and wholesale textile distributors. Underwriters must enforce these parameters without deviation unless an exception is formally approved by the Credit Committee under Clause 14.4.8.

### 14.4.2 Working Capital Cycle and Cash Flow Dynamics
1. The typical working capital cycle for the textile sector is **90 days**, measured from raw material procurement (cotton, synthetic yarn, fabric) to final cash realization from receivables.
2. Underwriters must base working capital facility limits on a maximum 90-day operating cycle calculation. Any borrower exhibiting a working capital cycle exceeding 115 days requires a mandatory forensic audit of inventory aging by Ashva Capital Risk Operations prior to facility renewal.

### 14.4.3 Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) Floor
1. The standard Ashva Capital DSCR floor of 1.25x is superseded in this sector. 
2. All textile sector SME applications must maintain a minimum **DSCR floor of 1.30x** calculated on projected cash flows available for debt service over the trailing twelve months and the next twelve months projected.

### 14.4.4 Seasonality Adjustments and Peak Working Capital
1. The textile sector experiences a defined seasonal peak from **August to October**, driven by pre-festive inventory accumulation and primary harvest arrivals.
2. During the seasonal peak window (August 1 to October 31), underwriters may approve a temporary, self-liquidating over-limit facility of up to 15% of the approved working capital limit, subject to a maximum bullet repayment deadline of November 30 of the same calendar year.
3. Historical financial statements submitted for underwriting must normalize seasonal spikes by averaging revenue and inventory across a full 12-month fiscal cycle rather than extrapolating peak-month performance.

### 14.4.5 Primary Causes of Sector Distress
Underwriters must specifically evaluate and mitigate the two most common structural causes of default in the textile SME segment:
1. **Volatile Raw Material Price Dislocation:** Unhedged exposure to raw cotton or petrochemical-based synthetic fiber price fluctuations that compress gross margins before finished goods pricing can be adjusted.
2. **Delayed Buyer Realizations and Channel Inventory Glut:** Payment defaults or extended credit terms forced by tier-1 apparel retailers and institutional buyers, leading to cash flow blockages at the SME level.

### 14.4.6 Acceptable Collateral and Valuation Haircuts
Collateral security must comprise tangible assets subject to the following mandatory valuation haircuts:
1. **Industrial Land and Buildings:** Maximum 30% discount to current independent valuer market value. First exclusive charge required.
2. **Plant and Machinery (Looms, Spinning Frames, Processing Units):** Maximum 50% discount to distressed liquidation value. Charge must be registered with the national collateral registry within 14 days of disbursement.
3. **Book Debts and Inventory:** Acceptable only for revolving working capital lines, subject to a 40% margin on raw materials/finished goods and exclusion of receivables aged over 90 days.

### 14.4.7 Portfolio Exposure Limits
1. Total credit exposure to the textile sector shall not exceed **15 percent of the total Ashva Capital loan book**.
2. As of the current reporting cycle, the active textile portfolio stands at **13.2 percent** of the loan book. Underwriters must verify live portfolio capacity with the Chief Risk Officer before issuing any Sanction Letter exceeding INR 25,000,000 in this sector.
3. The absolute **maximum tenor** for any term loan facility granted under this sector policy is **60 months**.

### 14.4.8 Mandatory Numeric Threshold Summary Table
For system validation and automated underwriting rules engine compliance, the following thresholds are hardcoded and immutable:

| Parameter | Mandated Threshold |
| :--- | :--- |
| DSCR Floor | **1.30x** |
| Typical Working Capital Cycle | **90 days** |
| Maximum Sector Exposure Cap | **15 percent** of loan book |
| Current Sector Exposure | **13.2 percent** of loan book |
| Maximum Facility Tenor | **60 months** |
| Seasonal Peak Window | **August to October** |

### 14.4.9 Exceptions and Governance
Any variance from the 1.30x DSCR floor, the 60-month maximum tenor, or the 15 percent exposure cap requires unanimous written sign-off from the Credit Committee, comprising the Chief Credit Officer, Chief Risk Officer, and Head of SME Lending. No regional credit manager possesses delegation authority to override these specific thresholds.
