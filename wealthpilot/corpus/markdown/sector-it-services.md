# Sector Underwriting Note: IT Services

**ASHVA CAPITAL INTERNAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL**
**SECTION 4: SECTOR-SPECIFIC UNDERWRITING PROTOCOLS**
**SUB-SECTION 4.12: INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY (IT) SERVICES SME LENDING**

---

### 4.12.1 Purpose and Scope
This sub-section establishes mandatory underwriting standards, financial covenants, and structural constraints for credit facilities extended to Small and Medium Enterprise (SME) borrowers operating within the Information Technology (IT) Services sector. All underwriters, credit committee members, and automated decision engines of Ashva Capital must adhere strictly to these provisions. Any deviation requires written sign-off from Chief Credit Officer Arthur Vance or Deputy Chief Credit Officer Brenda Sterling.

### 4.12.2 Sector Working Capital Cycle
1. Underwriters shall model the working capital cycle for IT Services SMEs using a baseline benchmark of **55 days** from invoice generation to cash collection.
2. The DSO (Days Sales Outstanding) component shall not exceed **75 days** on a trailing twelve-month (TTM) average basis. Accounts receivable aged greater than **90 days** from invoice date shall be excluded from the borrowing base calculation.

### 4.12.3 Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) Floor
1. The minimum acceptable Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) for IT Services SME borrowers is **1.25x**, matching the standard Ashva Capital baseline. 
2. Projections used to calculate the DSCR must incorporate principal and interest payments on all existing debt plus the proposed Ashva Capital facility, tested under a **15%** revenue contraction stress scenario.

### 4.12.4 Seasonality Adjustments
1. IT Services SMEs exhibit **no marked season** in revenue generation or cash flow velocity. 
2. Consequently, underwriters shall not apply seasonal downward adjustments to cash flow forecasts or debt service capacity calculations. Monthly debt service must be supported by run-rate operational cash flow rather than anticipated Q4 budget-flush surges.

### 4.12.5 Primary Causes of Distress
Underwriters must explicitly evaluate and mitigate the two most common structural causes of borrower distress in this sector:
1. **Client Concentration Risk:** Severe exposure where a single end-client accounts for more than **30%** of total annual billings. Mitigation requires mandatory credit insurance or a structural sweep of receivables if the primary client's credit rating drops below investment grade.
2. **Key-Person and Talent Attrition:** Margin erosion and revenue contraction resulting from the departure of billable senior engineering talent. Mitigation requires verification of non-compete covenants, key-man insurance policies, and a minimum bench strength of **15%** unallocated billable personnel.

### 4.12.6 Acceptable Collateral Types
Underwriting teams shall restrict collateral valuation to the following approved security types, subject to the stated advance rates:
1. First-ranking hypothecation over book debts and accounts receivable (maximum advance rate: **75%** of eligible receivables under **90 days**).
2. Fixed and floating charge over all current and movable fixed assets, including server hardware, office infrastructure, and intellectual property proceeds (maximum advance rate: **40%** of net book value).
3. Corporate guarantee from the ultimate parent entity or personal guarantees from founding promoters holding greater than **20%** equity stake, backed by unencumbered residential or commercial real estate collateral.

### 4.12.7 Portfolio Concentration Limits and Facility Parameters
1. **Sector Exposure Cap:** Total aggregate credit exposure to the IT Services sector shall not exceed **16.0%** of the total Ashva Capital loan book. As of the effective date of this manual, current sector exposure stands at **11.3%**, leaving a available headroom of **4.7%**.
2. **Maximum Tenor:** The maximum allowable repayment tenor for any term loan or revolving facility extended under this sector policy is **48 months**.
3. **Single Borrower Limit:** Maximum exposure to any single IT Services SME borrower group shall not exceed **$2,500,000** (Two Million Five Hundred Thousand US Dollars) or **5.0%** of total capital reserves, whichever is lower.
