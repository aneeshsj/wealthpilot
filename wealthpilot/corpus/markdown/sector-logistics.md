# Sector Underwriting Note: Logistics

**ASHVA CAPITAL INTERNAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL**
**SECTION 4: INDUSTRY-SPECIFIC UNDERWRITING PROTOCOLS**
**SUB-SECTION 4.8: LOGISTICS SME LENDING**

---

### 4.8.1 Scope and Application
This sub-section governs credit evaluation standards for Small and Medium Enterprise (SME) borrowers operating within the commercial logistics, freight forwarding, and warehousing sector. Underwriters bound by this manual must apply the specific thresholds, stress adjustments, and covenant structures detailed herein. Where logistics sector rules conflict with general SME lending standards, this sub-section shall take precedence.

### 4.8.2 Portfolio Concentration Limits
1. **Sector Exposure Cap:** Ashva Capital’s aggregate outstanding principal balance exposure to the logistics sector shall not exceed **10 percent** of the total Ashva Capital loan book.
2. **Current Portfolio Status:** The logistics sector portfolio is currently at its maximum exposure limit of **10 percent**.
3. **Automated Processing and Routing:**
   - Any new loan application originating from a logistics sector borrower received via the automated origination platform shall be assigned system reason code `SECTOR_CAP`.
   - The automated decision engine is strictly prohibited from issuing a conditional or unconditional approval for any application bearing reason code `SECTOR_CAP`.
   - All applications carrying reason code `SECTOR_CAP` shall be automatically routed to a designated human senior credit reviewer for manual exception evaluation, portfolio rebalancing assessment, or waitlist queue placement.

### 4.8.3 Working Capital Cycle and Cash Flow Dynamics
1. **Typical Working Capital Cycle:** The standard operating working capital cycle for qualified logistics SMEs is defined as **30 days**, measured from the date of freight dispatch or service delivery to cash collection from corporate shippers or 3PL principals.
2. **Cycle Variance Threshold:** Any borrower whose verified working capital cycle exceeds 45 days shall be subjected to an incremental 15% haircut on accounts receivable eligibility during borrowing base calculations.

### 4.8.4 Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) Floor
1. **Sector-Specific DSCR Floor:** While the general Ashva Capital SME credit policy mandates a baseline DSCR of 1.25x, the sector-specific DSCR floor for logistics SME lending is established at **1.40x**.
2. **Calculation Methodology:** The DSCR numerator shall consist of Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization (EBITDA) minus unfinanced capital expenditures and cash taxes. The denominator shall comprise all mandatory principal and interest debt service obligations over the ensuing 12-month projection period. No credit committee may approve a logistics facility with a projected base-case DSCR below **1.40x**.

### 4.8.5 Seasonality Adjustments
1. **Designated Peak Period:** The logistics sector exhibits high cyclicality, with the recognized seasonal peak window running annually from **September to December** (inclusive).
2. **Off-Peak Stress Testing:** Underwriters must model off-peak revenue contractions of up to 25% for the months of January through March.
3. **Seasonal Working Capital Lines:** Revolving facilities sized for the September to December peak must include an mandatory amortization or paydown clause requiring a zero-balance or reduced-draw status by March 31 of the subsequent calendar year.

### 4.8.6 Maximum Tenor Limit
1. **Facility Maturity:** The maximum allowable loan tenor for any term loan, equipment finance, or working capital line extended under the logistics sector protocol is **48 months**.
2. **Prohibition of Balloon Payments:** Tenors exceeding **48 months** require direct sign-off by Chief Credit Officer Marcus Vance and must incorporate fully amortizing structures without balloon maturities.

### 4.8.7 Common Causes of Sector Distress
Underwriters must explicitly evaluate and document mitigants against the two primary operational catalysts of default in the logistics SME sector:
1. **Fuel Price Volatility:** Unhedged spikes in diesel and maritime bunker fuel costs that compress operating margins faster than freight rate adjustments can be passed through to end-shippers. *Mitigant:* Requirement of a fuel surcharge adjustment clause in underlying shipper contracts or verified cash reserve balances equal to 4 weeks of peak fuel consumption.
2. **Customer Concentration and Contract Loss:** Over-reliance on a single freight broker, manufacturer, or retail principal accounting for more than 20% of gross top-line revenue. *Mitigant:* Mandatory assignment of receivables from the top three corporate accounts or maintenance of minimum quick ratios of 1.10x.

### 4.8.8 Acceptable Collateral Types and Valuation
Eligible collateral for logistics SME lending is restricted to the following asset classes, subject to the stated advance rates:
1. **Commercial Freight Fleet Vehicles:** Heavy-duty trucks, refrigerated trailers, and light commercial delivery vans (maximum age at loan origination: 6 years). *Maximum Advance Rate:* 70% of orderly liquidation value (OLV).
2. **Warehouse Material Handling Equipment:** Forklifts, automated sorting machinery, and pallet racking systems. *Maximum Advance Rate:* 60% of OLV.
3. **B2B Trade Accounts Receivable:** Invoices due from investment-grade or pre-approved Tier-2 corporate shippers aged under 60 days. *Maximum Advance Rate:* 80% of eligible receivables (subject to standard 90-day dilution and contra-account reserves).
