# Sector Underwriting Note: Auto Components

**ASHVA CAPITAL INTERNAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL**
**SECTION 9.4: SECTOR UNDERWRITING NOTE – AUTO COMPONENTS SME LENDING**

---

### 9.4.1 PURPOSE AND APPLICABILITY
This policy governs the credit evaluation, risk grading, and exposure management for Small and Medium Enterprise (SME) borrowers operating in the Indian auto components manufacturing sector. All underwriters, credit managers, and members of the Credit Committee of Ashva Capital are strictly bound by the thresholds and criteria detailed herein. Deviations require approval per Section 2 of the Delegated Authority Matrix.

### 9.4.2 WORKING CAPITAL CYCLE DYNAMICS
1. The typical operating working capital cycle for Tier-2 and Tier-3 auto component manufacturers spans **80 days**, calculated from raw material procurement (crude steel, aluminum ingots, rubber compounds) to cash realization from Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs) or Tier-1 vendors.
2. Inventory holding periods average 35 days, receivable collection periods average 42 days, and payable stretch periods average 27 days. Any applicant exhibiting a cash conversion cycle exceeding 115 days must be classified as High Risk and requires dual sign-off from the Head of Credit Risk, Marcus Vance.

### 9.4.3 DEBT SERVICE COVERAGE RATIO (DSCR) FLOOR
1. Standard SME credit mandates a baseline DSCR of 1.25x. However, owing to margin compression risks arising from raw material volatility and OEM price renegotiations, the sector-specific **DSCR floor is set at 1.35x** across the entire facility tenure.
2. Projections must account for a 15% stress test on EBITDA. Facilities failing to maintain a 1.35x DSCR post-stress shall be declined outright without escalation.

### 9.4.4 SEASONALITY ADJUSTMENTS AND CASH FLOWS
1. The auto components sector exhibits pronounced seasonal peaks between **July and September**, driven by pre-festive manufacturing ramp-ups by OEMs. 
2. During the lean period (April to June), drawing power limits on revolving Working Capital Demand Loans (WCDL) shall be automatically trimmed by 15% based on stock audit valuations. 
3. Underwriters must ensure that peak-season cash surpluses are escrowed to service term loan debt obligations falling due in the subsequent low-demand quarter.

### 9.4.5 PRIMARY CAUSES OF DISTRESS
Underwriters must explicitly evaluate and document mitigants for the two primary failure modes unique to this sector:
1. **OEM Concentration Risk:** Over-reliance on a single anchor OEM, where a production cut or model failure at the OEM level immediately impairs the borrower's debt servicing capacity. Any borrower deriving more than 65% of net revenues from a single OEM must maintain a minimum cash-backed Debt Service Reserve Account (DSRA) equal to 3 months of principal and interest.
2. **Tooling and Capex Misalignment:** Funding long-term tooling assets using short-term working capital lines, leading to chronic liquidity mismatches. Term loans must be structured with a **maximum tenor of 60 months** to align with OEM product lifecycle contracts.

### 9.4.6 ACCEPTABLE COLLATERAL AND MARGINS
Collateral security packages must consist of the following eligible assets, valued by an empaneled surveyor not more than 45 days prior to sanction:
1. **Primary Security:** Hypothecation of plant, machinery, and current assets (raw materials, work-in-progress, finished goods, and book debts) with a minimum security margin of 25%.
2. **Collateral Security:** Equitable mortgage on industrial land and factory buildings with a minimum margin of 40% (loan-to-value cap of 60%).
3. **Personal Guarantees:** Unconditional and irrevocable personal guarantees of all promoter-directors holding greater than 20% equity stake. Third-party corporate guarantees are inadmissible unless backed by tangible fixed assets.

### 9.4.7 PORTFOLIO EXPOSURE LIMITS
1. Ashva Capital’s aggregate loan book exposure to the Auto Components SME sector is capped at a **maximum exposure of 13 percent of the loan book**.
2. As of the current reporting cycle, the active portfolio stands at **12.1 percent**. 
3. Systemic triggers are pre-programmed into the Ashva Lending Engine:
   * **Yellow Alert (12.5%):** Requires weekly portfolio monitoring by Portfolio Risk Director Sarah Lin.
   * **Red Alert (13.0%):** Immediate freeze on all new fresh sanctions in this sector, applicable until repayments reduce the portfolio share below 11.5%.
