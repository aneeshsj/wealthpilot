# Sector Underwriting Note: Light Engineering

**ASHVA CAPITAL INTERNAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL**  
**SECTION 4.2: SECTOR UNDERWRITING NOTE – LIGHT ENGINEERING SME LENDING**  
*Classification: Internal Risk & Underwriting Directive*  
*Effective Date: 1.10.2024*  

---

### 4.2.1 Purpose and Scope
This note establishes the mandatory underwriting parameters, risk tolerances, and evaluation criteria for credit facilities extended to Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) operating within the Light Engineering sector. Underwriters must adhere strictly to the quantitative thresholds and qualitative constraints detailed herein. Deviations require approval from the Credit Committee chaired by Chief Risk Officer Marcus Vance.

### 4.2.2 Sector Working Capital Cycle
1. Underwriters shall calculate the working capital cycle using the standard formula: **[Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) + Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO) - Days Payables Outstanding (DPO)]**.
2. The benchmark working capital cycle for the Light Engineering sector is **75 days**.
3. Any borrower exhibiting a working capital cycle exceeding **95 days** shall trigger a mandatory operational audit by the Ashva Capital Risk Operations Unit prior to facility sanction.

### 4.2.3 Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) Floor
1. Due to the capital-intensive replacement cycles and margin volatility inherent in light engineering manufacturing, the standard portfolio DSCR floor of 1.25x is superseded for this sector.
2. The mandatory minimum **DSCR floor is 1.35x** based on projected cash flows over the trailing twelve months (TTM) and the next twelve months (NTM).
3. Facilities with a projected DSCR between **1.30x and 1.34x** may only be considered if the borrower maintains a minimum unencumbered cash buffer equal to 6 months of debt service in an escrow account held with Ashva Capital.

### 4.2.4 Seasonality Adjustments
1. The Light Engineering sector experiences distinct cyclical demand, with the primary **seasonal peak occurring from January to March**.
2. During the seasonal peak window (January to March), working capital revolving facilities may experience a temporary drawing limit increase of up to **20 percent**, provided the borrower demonstrates historical cash conversion efficiency of at least **85 percent** during the preceding peak period.
3. Underwriters must model cash flow projections using a 12-month rolling average to smooth out the January to March revenue spike, ensuring debt service calculations are not distorted by peak-month inflows.

### 4.2.5 Primary Causes of Distress
Underwriters must specifically evaluate and mitigate the two most common root causes of default in the Light Engineering sector:
1. **Raw Material Price Volatility:** Unhedged exposure to fluctuations in non-ferrous metals, industrial fasteners, and specialty alloy pricing. Borrowers must demonstrate a documented inventory pricing pass-through mechanism or fixed-price supply contracts extending a minimum of **90 days**.
2. **Machinery Obsolescence and Maintenance Drag:** Unexpected capital expenditure requirements to maintain aging CNC machinery and fabrication equipment. Underwriters must deduct a mandatory maintenance capital expenditure (CapEx) reserve of **3.5 percent** of gross annual revenue from operating cash flow calculations.

### 4.2.6 Acceptable Collateral Types and Valuation Haircuts
Facilities must be secured by a combination of the following eligible collateral types, registered under the Ashva Capital Security Registry:
1. **Commercial Real Estate (Industrial Land and Factory Sheds):** Valued by an accredited Ashva Capital panel valuer. Maximum loan-to-value (LTV) cap is **65 percent**.
2. **Plant and Machinery (New or Refurbished under 5 years):** First exclusive charge over movable fixed assets. Maximum LTV cap is **50 percent**.
3. **Book Debts and Inventory:** Floating charge over current assets, restricted to book debts under **90 days** and raw material inventory. Maximum LTV cap is **40 percent**.

### 4.2.7 Portfolio Exposure Caps and Tenor Limits
1. The aggregate maximum exposure to the Light Engineering sector shall not exceed **14 percent of the total Ashva Capital loan book**.
2. As of the effective date of this manual, the current portfolio exposure stands at **11.5 percent**, leaving a remaining sector capacity of **2.5 percent**. Underwriters must verify available sector headroom via the credit system dashboard prior to issuing any Sanction Letter.
3. The absolute **maximum tenor for term loans in this sector is 72 months**, inclusive of any initial principal moratorium period. Working capital facilities shall be subject to annual review and renewal, not exceeding a 12-month validity per tranche.
